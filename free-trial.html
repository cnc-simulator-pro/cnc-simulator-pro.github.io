CNC Simulator Pro free trial: what you get and what happened to the free version

Searching for a CNC Simulator Pro free download usually means one of two things: the trial of the current version, or the old free licence. The trial is still there; the free licences ended in January 2026, and legacy version 3.2 remains only for older PCs.

CNC Simulator Pro is not free as a full product, but there is a free trial. The installer from the official download page runs as a trial when no licence is entered, which the vendor's help desk describes as a full 30 day trial. How the CNC Simulator Pro free trial works

The CNC Simulator Pro free trial is not a separate download. The download page states that a trial is included if you do not have a licence, so you install the same program that paying users get and simply skip activation. The help desk describes it as a full trial lasting 30 days.

Because the trial and the paid program are one installer, nothing has to be reinstalled later. When you buy, you enter the licence code in the program and keep your settings, programs and custom items. The pricing page explains the two licence types and what each includes.

What happened to the free version

For years many users ran the simulator on a free licence, and plenty of old forum posts still say it is free. The vendor's help desk states that free licences were discontinued after 1 January 2026. Instructions you find for reactivating a free licence refer to that older arrangement.

The trial remains the official way to use the current version without paying. Downloads offered as a cracked or unlocked copy are not the vendor's build, are not signed by the publisher and carry real risk on a workshop or school PC. Use only the official installer.

Legacy version 3.2

The download page also offers version 3.2 as a 32 bit installer. The vendor describes it as lighter and better suited to older computers with a 32 bit processor, a weak graphics chip or little memory. It is no longer updated, and the online help has a separate manual for it.

Choose 3.2 only if version 4 will not run on your hardware. Check the system requirements first; a driver update or a better graphics card often solves the problem without falling back to an unmaintained release.

What to check during the trial

Thirty days is enough to answer the questions that matter before paying. Use them on the PC and the programs you will really use.

  1. Load a program you already know works on a real machine and confirm the toolpath looks the same.
  2. Pick a machine from the library that is close to yours and check its axis limits against the vendor's machine catalog.
  3. Try SimCam on a simple part to see whether the built in CAD/CAM covers your needs.
  4. Watch the frame rate on a long program; version 4 leans on the graphics card.
  5. Go through one tutorial or example to judge the teaching material if you plan to use it in class.

If the program passes these checks, the review covers the remaining limits, such as the four axis maximum on mills.

When the trial ends

After the trial you need a licence to keep using the current version. The help desk lists an annual subscription with updates and a perpetual licence without free updates. If neither fits, a free open source simulator may cover simple 3 axis work, though it will not match the machine range or the training material.

Frequently asked questions

Is CNC Simulator Pro free to download?

The installer is free to download from the official site and runs as a trial without a licence. Continued use after the trial needs a paid licence. The free licences of earlier years ended on 1 January 2026.

How long is the trial?

The vendor's help desk describes a full 30 day trial. The download page itself only says that a free trial is included if you do not have a licence.

Do I need to reinstall after buying?

No. The trial and the paid program use the same installer. You enter the licence code in the program and continue with your files.

Is version 3.2 still free?

Version 3.2 is still offered on the download page as a 32 bit legacy installer, but it is no longer updated. The free licences that once came with it were discontinued after 1 January 2026, according to the help desk.

Is a cracked copy a safe way to try it?

No. Only the official installer is signed by the publisher Zyntaxis. Modified copies can carry malware and break the licence agreement, which matters on school and workshop PCs.
