What CNC Simulator Pro does

CNC Simulator Pro is a virtual CNC workshop for Windows. You load or write a G code program, pick one of the simulated machines, and watch the tool move through a block of virtual material before any real spindle turns. The aim is to catch a wrong offset, a crash into a clamp or a missed feed before it costs a tool, a workpiece or a machine repair.

The library holds more than 40 machines in five categories: mills from 2 to 4 axes, lathes, cutters for laser, plasma and waterjet work, and 3D printers. They range from large industrial models to small hobby routers. A virtual controller runs the code, collision checks flag contact the program should not make, and materials and effects are drawn to look close to the real process.

The program also ships SimCam, a built in CAD/CAM system with 2D and 3D tools, so you can draw a part, generate a toolpath and simulate it in the same window. When the stock items do not match your shop, you can define your own workpieces, tools and materials. For a scored look at the whole package and its limits, read the full review.

Who it is made for

The company names three groups. Workshops use it to verify programs before they reach the machine. Schools and training centres use it to teach G code without putting students in front of a live spindle. Hobbyists use it to try macros and custom cycles safely.

The training focus shows in the extras around the program: a quick reference for G codes, M codes and special commands, a CNC Academy with tutorials and videos, a machine catalog that lists each model with images and axis limits, and free online generators for milling and turning setup programs and for workpieces.

It is not built to be a digital twin of a specific five axis machine with its exact kinematics; the mills in the library go to four axes. If you mainly mill on a 3 axis hobby router, a free tool may be enough, and the comparison with CAMotics sets out where each one fits.

What it costs and how to try it

The vendor sells two licence types: an annual subscription that includes every update released during the year, and a perpetual licence for a one time fee that does not include free updates afterwards. The amounts, the refund terms the company publishes and the ones it does not are set out on the pricing and licence page.

You do not have to pay to look. The current installer runs as a free trial when no licence is entered. The old version 3.2 is still offered for weak 32 bit PCs, but it no longer gets updates. When you are ready, the download page explains what the installer contains and the setup steps, including the Windows warning that often appears after a new release.

What your PC needs

The installer is Windows only. Version 4 draws far more 3D detail than earlier releases, so the graphics card matters more than raw processor speed.

Frequently asked questions

What is CNC Simulator Pro?

It is a Windows program that simulates CNC machines so you can run and check G code programs on screen. It includes more than 40 machines in five categories, a virtual controller, collision checks and the SimCam CAD/CAM system.

Is CNC Simulator Pro free?

Not as a full product. The installer includes a free trial, and the company sells an annual subscription and a perpetual licence. The old version 3.2 can still be downloaded but no longer receives updates.

Does it run on a Mac?

No. The download page lists Windows 10 or later in 64 bit with Microsoft .NET Framework 4.8 or higher. Mac users would need a Windows installation or a different simulator.

Which machines can it simulate?

Mills with 2 to 4 axes, lathes, laser, plasma and waterjet cutters, and 3D printers, from industrial machines down to hobby CNCs. The vendor's machine catalog lists each model with images and axis limits.

Is it suitable for students?

Yes, training is one of the main uses the vendor names. The program comes with tutorials and example programs, and the support site adds a CNC Academy, a codes quick reference and online setup generators.
